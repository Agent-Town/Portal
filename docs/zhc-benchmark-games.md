# ZHC Benchmark Games

Status: reference shortlist for ZHC0 design and product benchmarking  
Last updated: 2026-03-16
Branch: `zhc0-founders-loop`

## 1. Short answer

There are **adjacent games**, but there is not really a mainstream game that cleanly does:

> start a company with your AI agent, automate it, save memory, and grow it into a zero-human company.

That gap is the opportunity.

So the right move is not to copy one game.
It is to build a **reference stack** from several genres.

---

## 2. Best benchmark categories

We should borrow from four buckets:

1. **company / tycoon games**
2. **automation / delegation games**
3. **AI / systems explanation games**
4. **world / room / HQ games**

---

## 3. Strongest benchmark games

## 3.1 Software Inc.

### Why it matters
Probably the strongest benchmark for:

- software-company fantasy
- office/HQ layout
- specialist roles
- scaling from founder to team
- process and management depth

### What to borrow
- HQ feeling
- room/office semantics
- role specialization over time
- company growth feeling

### What not to copy
- too much spreadsheet/admin density too early
- too much labor-management overhead in the first hour

### Usefulness for ZHC0
Very high for:
- House as HQ
- later multi-worker company design

---

## 3.2 Startup Company

### Why it matters
Good benchmark for:

- online business / SaaS framing
- product + marketing + support + growth loop
- feeling like a real digital business

### What to borrow
- market/progress feedback
- online-business framing
- feature/build/launch/grow rhythm

### What not to copy
- generic startup-dashboard blandness
- KPI soup before the player cares

### Usefulness for ZHC0
High for:
- first business loop
- later market/growth loops

---

## 3.3 Game Dev Tycoon / Game Dev Story

### Why it matters
These are excellent benchmarks for:

- simple first playable loop
- beginner-friendly progression
- idea -> build -> launch -> feedback rhythm
- making business-building feel approachable

### What to borrow
- clean first-run pacing
- obvious progression
- market feedback after a launch/action
- a loop that is easy to understand fast

### What not to copy
- oversimplified hidden-number design
- pure abstraction with no operational reality

### Usefulness for ZHC0
Very high for:
- the first playable loop structure
- tutorial pacing

---

## 3.4 Good Company

### Why it matters
Good benchmark for:

- process design
- workflow scaling
- operational bottlenecks
- turning a small operation into a repeatable system

### What to borrow
- process thinking
- scaling workflows over time
- turning ad hoc work into repeatable operations

### What not to copy
- overcomplicated logistics too early
- making the player micromanage diagrams before the core fantasy lands

### Usefulness for ZHC0
Very high for:
- Workshop
- SOP/process design
- scaling from founder hustle to systemized business

---

## 3.5 Autonauts

### Why it matters
One of the best benchmarks for:

- teaching workers to do repeatable jobs
- delegating labor
- making automation understandable and playful

### What to borrow
- “teach workers, then let them repeat” feeling
- automation as relief and empowerment
- workers as understandable helpers, not black boxes

### What not to copy
- toy-like tone if it undermines the company fantasy
- too much step-by-step rote scripting in the main loop

### Usefulness for ZHC0
Extremely high for:
- worker delegation fantasy
- later multi-agent company behavior

---

## 3.6 while True: learn()

### Why it matters
Probably the strongest benchmark for:

- AI/ML-flavored work made legible to non-experts
- packaging technical ideas into approachable interactions

### What to borrow
- making AI-ish work understandable without deep AI fluency
- friendly explanation of technical process
- puzzle-like but meaningful structure

### What not to copy
- leaning too hard into abstract node/pipeline toyification
- replacing the company/world fantasy with pure puzzle boards

### Usefulness for ZHC0
Very high for:
- Lite/brain onboarding
- first mission explanation
- AI capability framing without jargon overload

---

## 3.7 Factorio / shapez

### Why it matters
These are the gold standard for:

- automation clarity
- system expansion
- throughput thinking
- bottleneck visibility

### What to borrow
- automation should feel powerful and compounding
- bottlenecks should become legible
- scaling should feel earned

### What not to copy
- factory-graph obsession in the first user experience
- making the product feel like pure logistics instead of company-building

### Usefulness for ZHC0
High for:
- long-term automation philosophy
- workflow scaling
- later optimization loops

---

## 3.8 Big Ambitions

### Why it matters
Good benchmark for:

- grounded business fantasy in a navigable world
- business-building feeling tied to place and life

### What to borrow
- “you are in a world building something real”
- business ambition linked to lived environment

### What not to copy
- too much sim-life overhead
- too much realism friction unrelated to the core agent/company fantasy

### Usefulness for ZHC0
Medium-high for:
- world grounding
- making Agent Town feel like a place, not just menus

---

## 3.9 Screeps

### Why it matters
Niche but very relevant benchmark for:

- programmable autonomous workers in a persistent world
- player-written logic driving ongoing behavior

### What to borrow
- agents as persistent workers
- ongoing operation in a living environment
- code/logic as leverage

### What not to copy
- full programmer-only barrier
- assuming every player wants to live in code

### Usefulness for ZHC0
High for:
- long-term serious agent-company direction
- portability/self-host evolution

---

## 4. The best synthesis for Agent Town

If I had to compress the best references into one product formula:

> **Game Dev Tycoon** for the first-loop pacing,
> **Software Inc.** for HQ/company depth,
> **Autonauts** for worker delegation,
> **while True: learn()** for AI legibility,
> **Good Company** for workflow scaling,
> and a little **Big Ambitions** for world grounding.

That’s the strongest blend.

## 6. What not to benchmark against too hard

Avoid drifting too far toward:

1. pure spreadsheet capitalism sims
2. idle-clicker fake startup games
3. overly abstract management dashboards
4. survival colony chaos as the main fantasy
5. devtool products disguised as games

The goal is:
- real company fantasy,
- real automation fantasy,
- real agent partnership,
- simple enough first hour.

---

## 7. Recommendation

For ZHC0, the strongest practical reference stack is:

1. **Game Dev Tycoon** — first playable loop
2. **Software Inc.** — HQ/company depth
3. **Autonauts** — worker delegation logic
4. **while True: learn()** — approachable AI framing
5. **Good Company** — process/system scaling

That’s the benchmark bundle I would actively design against.
