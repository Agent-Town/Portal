# Phase D2 Evidence

Status: Captured on 2026-03-16

This folder contains the before and after screenshots for the D2 implementation:

1. `/start` at mobile, tablet, and desktop
2. `/app` town hub at mobile, tablet, and desktop

## Comparison notes

### `/start`

Before:

1. the eye landed on the media block first,
2. the page felt dependent on a third-party embed,
3. the warning competed with the action.

After:

1. the eye lands on the title and `Enter` button first,
2. the media area now reads as supporting context,
3. the fallback overlay makes the page feel intentional even if the media is slow or blocked,
4. the warning reads as a quiet caution instead of a competing demand.

Why the hierarchy is better:

1. the first screen now answers "what is this?" and "what do I do?" before anything else,
2. a user does not need AI or product vocabulary to understand the next step,
3. the primary action remains visible at mobile size without scrolling.

### `/app`

Before:

1. the town looked atmospheric before it looked interactive,
2. not every district label was visible at a glance,
3. the user had to infer what would open next.

After:

1. the eye lands on a short guidance HUD, then on the labeled districts,
2. each major district has a visible tappable label,
3. the selected-state support copy is clearer and more human-facing,
4. mobile still leaves the map visible instead of covering it with oversized HUD cards.

Why the hierarchy is better:

1. the town now reads as a set of places to tap, not just a background image,
2. the top copy explains the world without forcing the user into AI or system language,
3. the design remains compatible with international and future voice-oriented interaction because the places and actions are short, named, and visible.
